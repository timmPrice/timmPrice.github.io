Shinkansen Home-lab

Dev Log 0 · Initial Setup and Planning

updated: 2026-11-17

⮘ back home ⮚

As I transition from a career as a student to managing I.T. infrastructure full time, I need a place to continue learning, outside of work, where it is safe to build and break new technology. I’m moving on from trying to build a perfectly optimized system for software development and writing LaTeX/Typst now that class is over (see my system). I previously started a Proxmox server last spring with the hopes to study Red Hat system administration topics for the RHCSA but got busy during my internship over the summer and haven’t touched it since. My end-game here is to create a lab for the technologies that I don’t always get to use at work, and to build automated provisioning and maintenance from scratch. At work most of this was already in place when I arrived. I’d like to take that practice and one day move those ideas into production somewhere.

It's common to build home-labs that serve as a lower-stakes practice environment. They can automate parts of our lives and can replace SaaS services and subscriptions via a related hobby “self-hosting.” In general home-labs are fun, never-ending projects that tend to balloon in scale quickly. This Dev-Log series will keep up closely with what I’m building and hosting in mine! The rest of this article will detail the initial groundwork and conventions that will help organize my lab early on.

Shinkansen Theme (Hardware Host Machines)


fig 1 · sweet A.I. generated Shinkansen train I found on the inter·webs

I want to architect a lab that is unique. While this is a learning environment foremost, I also want a well-organized and reliable setup. This organizational idea is embodied well by the Shinkansen train system which runs through Japan. Initially I plan on using the different tiers of trains to logically name the different hardware components of my lab (excluding networking equipment).

The service tiers (as of writing) are as follows:

Tier Service Hardware class
nozomi Fastest service Full-size desktop
hikari Middle tier Mini PC or similar
kodama Stops at every station Laptop

Since I have multiple laptops, for example I will need a second identifier for each device. So, a 4-letter name will be appended to the end of each device name. My Panasonic Let’s Note for example will be called “kodama-lets” for example, and my desktop will be named nozomi-desk, etc.

Guest VM/Container Naming Scheme

Having dedicated hardware for each individual system/service I want to integrate into my lab would be expensive. My initial plans involve 10+ guest additions into the lab’s network. Since there will be duplicate services or containers running in parallel, remembering names and the IPv4 addresses attached might be difficult. I didn’t want to do something like “rhel-0001” and “rhel-0002” which gets confusing fast and leaves room for lazily naming a new guest. Using the last octet of a /24 subnet (10.9.20.0/24) I’ll have access to a range of 1-254 addresses. This lines up nicely with atomic numbers ranging from 1-118. I got the idea from this post, thought it was cool and wanted to incorporate in my lab somehow.


fig 2 · atomic lab addressing

Guest Element Atomic # Address
wrt-hydrogen Hydrogen 1 10.9.20.1 (gateway)
ubu-carbon Carbon 6 10.9.20.6
nix-uranium Uranium 92 10.9.20.92

Similar to the hardware naming convention, I’ll prepend a 3-letter operating system “tag” to better identify the guest’s purpose. Since there are 118 elements the guest range will span from 2-118 statically set addresses and for host devices on the network starting at address 119-254 will be DHCP reservations in OpenWrt (wrt-hydrogen).

This is strictly being followed as I initially set up and provision the first host and guest machines, but is subject to revision if it gets too complicated. Remembering the philosophy of → Function > Form because this is admittedly complex at first glance.
